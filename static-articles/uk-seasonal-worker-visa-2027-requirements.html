UK Seasonal Worker Visa 2027: Complete Guide for Nigerians

By Mindnewsng Desk Last reviewed against GOV.UK: 24 Nov 2026 Next review: 1 Dec 2026

Quick-answer overview

The Seasonal Worker visa is a temporary work route for individuals aged 18 or over to undertake seasonal work in the UK's horticulture or poultry production sectors. It is a strictly short-term route that does not lead to settlement, and dependants are not permitted to join you.

FeatureHorticulturePoultry Production
Work windowUp to 6 months in any rolling 10-month period, at any time of year.Only between 2 October and 31 December each year.
Application deadlineCan apply at any time of year.Must apply on or before 15 November each year.
Maximum stay6 months.2 October to 31 December.

For 2026, the Home Office has allocated 42,900 places in total: 41,000 for horticulture and 1,900 for poultry.

The 22 eligible job areas (10 horticulture + 12 poultry)

The "22 jobs" figure refers to 22 eligible job categories or work areas, not 22 individual job titles. These are split between the two sectors covered by the visa: 10 in horticulture and 12 in poultry production.

🌱 Horticulture: 10 eligible work areas

Under the horticulture sector, the work must involve "growing" one of the following categories. This includes the cultivation and production of crops, fruits, flowers, and plants.

  1. Protected vegetables – those grown in glasshouse systems.
  2. Field vegetables – grown outdoors, including vegetables, herbs, leafy salads, and potatoes.
  3. Soft fruit – grown outdoors or under cover (e.g., in polytunnels), including strawberries, raspberries, blackcurrants, and blueberries.
  4. Top fruit (orchard fruit) – trees that bear fruit, such as apples, plums, cherries, and apricots.
  5. Vines and bines – climbing or twining plants, such as grapes (vines) and hops (bines).
  6. Mushrooms – typically Agaricus bisporus species but can also include more exotic species grown indoors.
  7. Bulbs and cut flowers – such as daffodils, grown both outdoors and indoors.
  8. Pot plants – such as seasonal bedding plants like pansies, violas, geraniums, and poinsettias.
  9. Hardy ornamental nursery plants – such as Christmas trees, shrubs, roses, ornamental trees, and perennials.
  10. Tree and forest nurseries.

🐔 Poultry production: 12 eligible roles

For the poultry sector, the work must be one of the specific roles listed below. These span multiple stages of the production chain, from handling live birds to preparing and packing meat.

  1. Butcher (SOC 2020 occupation code 5431)
  2. Bird or game dresser (SOC 2020 occupation code 5433)
  3. Killer and plucker (SOC 2020 occupation code 5433)
  4. Plucker (SOC 2020 occupation code 5433)
  5. Poulterer (SOC 2020 occupation code 5433)
  6. Poultry processor (SOC 2020 occupation code 5433)
  7. Poultry sticker (SOC 2020 occupation code 5433)
  8. Trusser (SOC 2020 occupation code 5433)
  9. Food operative (SOC 2020 occupation code 8111)
  10. Poultry catcher or handler (SOC 2020 occupation code 9111)
  11. Poultry vaccinator (SOC 2020 occupation code 9119)
  12. Poultry meat packer (SOC 2020 occupation code 9134)

💡 Important note on eligibility

Having a job that falls into one of these 22 categories is not sufficient on its own to qualify for the visa. You must also have a confirmed job offer from a Home Office-approved scheme operator (a sponsor) before you can apply.

The £340 vs. £1,270: fee vs. maintenance funds (crucial distinction)

A common point of confusion is the difference between the application fee and the maintenance requirement. They are two completely separate financial obligations.

ItemAmountWhat it isPaid to Home Office?
Application fee£340The non-refundable fee to process your visa application. This is the only fee you should pay.Yes — paid online when you submit.
Maintenance funds£1,270A personal savings requirement to prove you can support yourself after arriving in the UK. This money stays in your bank account.No — you must show you have it; you do not pay it to anyone.

Key rules for the £1,270 maintenance funds

  • You must have at least £1,270 in your bank account.
  • The money must have been held for 28 consecutive days.
  • Day 28 of that period must be within 31 days of your application date.
  • Exception: If your sponsor certifies maintenance on your Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS), you do not need to show the £1,270 yourself.

⚠ Critical warning: the Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS)

The Certificate of Sponsorship is the cornerstone of your application, but it is widely misunderstood. A mistake here almost guarantees refusal.

  • It is NOT a paper document. A CoS is a reference number held in a Home Office database. It contains your personal details and the job information.
  • It is single-use only. You can use the CoS reference number in one visa application. Once it has been used in an application that is either granted or refused, it cannot be used again.
  • It is time-limited. The CoS is only valid for 3 months from the date it is assigned to you. Your application must be submitted within this window.
  • It is void if the sponsor's licence is revoked. If the scheme operator's sponsor licence is revoked, any CoS they have assigned automatically becomes invalid. Any application based on that CoS will be refused.
  • Inconsistencies are fatal. If UKVI identifies discrepancies between the details on your CoS (e.g., working hours, pay, accommodation) and your actual contract or other evidence, your visa may be refused, and a compliance investigation may follow.

Documents you must provide

When you apply, you must submit the following:

  1. Your Certificate of Sponsorship reference number — provided by your sponsor.
  2. A valid passport or other travel document — proving your identity and nationality.
  3. Evidence of personal savings — for example, bank statements showing at least £1,270 held for 28 days. This is not required if your CoS confirms your sponsor certifies maintenance.

Additional points:

  • If any document is not in English or Welsh, you must provide a certified translation. The only exception is your passport.
  • The Home Office may request additional documents after you apply, so ensure you can provide further evidence if needed.

How to apply: 7 steps

  1. Secure a job offer with an approved scheme operator. You cannot apply directly to a farm; you must be recruited by a Home Office-approved scheme operator that is DEFRA-endorsed and GLAA-licensed.
  2. Receive your Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS). Your sponsor will assign a CoS to you and provide you with the reference number. Check all details on it for accuracy immediately.
  3. Complete the online application. Apply on GOV.UK using the specified form for a Temporary Worker visa. You can apply up to 3 months before your job start date.
  4. Pay the £340 application fee. This is paid online as part of the application.
  5. Prove your identity. Depending on your nationality and passport type, you will either:
    • Use the 'UK Immigration: ID Check' app to scan your document and create a UKVI account, or
    • Attend a visa application centre to provide fingerprints and a photograph.
  6. Upload your documents. Submit your CoS reference, passport, and financial evidence (if applicable).
  7. Await a decision. Once your identity is verified and documents are submitted, you will usually receive a decision within 3 weeks.

Frequently asked questions

1. Can I bring my family with me on a Seasonal Worker visa?

No. The Seasonal Worker route does not permit any dependants (partners or children) to join you.

2. Can I switch to another visa while in the UK on a Seasonal Worker visa?

The guidance does not indicate that switching is permitted in-country. This route is designed as a short-term, temporary entry route. You would generally need to leave the UK and apply from abroad for any other visa category for which you qualify.

3. Does the Seasonal Worker visa lead to permanent settlement (Indefinite Leave to Remain)?

No. The Seasonal Worker route is not a route to settlement.

4. Do I need to speak English to qualify?

No. There is no English language requirement for the Seasonal Worker visa.

5. Can I take a second job or work extra hours?

No. You can only work in the job described in your Certificate of Sponsorship. You cannot take a second job or a different job that is not stated on your CoS.

6. What happens if I have a child while I am in the UK on this visa?

Your child will not automatically become a British citizen. You must apply online for their visa as your dependant if you wish to travel in and out of the UK with them. You will need to provide a full UK birth certificate showing both parents' names.

7. Can my sponsor charge me any other fees?

No. You should only pay the £340 visa application fee. Sponsors are prohibited from passing on sponsorship fees and associated administrative costs to workers. You should not be charged any other recruitment or agency fees.

8. How long does it take to get a decision?

Once you have applied online, proved your identity, and provided your documents, you will usually receive a decision within 3 weeks. You may be able to pay for a faster decision in some locations.
